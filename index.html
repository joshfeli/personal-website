About Me

My approach to learning

I studied at MIT and gained a Bachelor's in Electrical Engineering and Computer Science in 2024. Throughout my time there, I've learned that I enjoy building things with code, but I've also discovered that I love teaching students how to do the same.

It gives me great pleasure to guide students through solving a problem, whether or not I know how to solve it by the time I sit down with them. I've encountered many students who don't yet believe in themselves, but I think that is largely due to them not knowing where to start. Technical instruction, I feel, should not just teach students what to solve, but also how to go about solving it.

Growing up in New York, I've been able to interact with students from diverse backgrounds, which impacts not just the food they eat or the music they enjoy but also how students think about problems. It often takes some translation—sometimes in spoken language, most often in thought—to convey ideas effectively, for an explanation that works for one student has no guarantee to satisfy the next.

When did I start learning how to solve problems and express myself confidently to a wide range of audiences? Much of it started at All Star Code's summer intensive back in 2018. There I was introduced to programming, giving an elevator pitch and, most importantly, learning to fail. It is that mindset that carried me during my time at MIT, and it is that mindset I hope to convey wherever I go.
